    How this map works

    Every crime from data.police.uk is placed into a hexagon and multiplied by a harm weight, so one robbery counts for more than one shoplifting. Hexagons are equal in area everywhere, so a rural hexagon and a city hexagon are directly comparable.

    A hotspot is a group of hexagons whose combined harm is far higher than chance would produce, tested with the Getis-Ord Gi* statistic and corrected for testing thousands of areas at once (1% false discovery rate).

    Harm weights (illustrative, editable in the pipeline)

    What this map can't tell you

    • It shows crime that was reported and recorded, not all crime, and not your personal risk.
    • Busy places like city centres and shopping streets rank high partly because many people pass through them; the counts are not divided by footfall or population.
    • Police publish approximate locations, snapped to the nearest of a fixed set of points, so street-level detail is only accurate to about a street.
    • Police Scotland does not publish to data.police.uk, so Scotland shows only British Transport Police records.
